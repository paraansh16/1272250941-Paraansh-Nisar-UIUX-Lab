# CCA3 Conclusion Report – Supreme: Original vs Modified Prototype

## 1. Method
5 participants (Archita, Aanvi, Siddhi, Aditya, Nisarg) recorded audio reviews comparing the original Supreme website with the Figma prototype, then were asked to complete the UX survey. Transcripts were produced with speech-to-text and read for recurring themes.

## 2. Quantitative results
All 5 participants were asked to complete the UX survey (https://forms.gle/5jm4o9pejXpaw6EN9) rating the original website and the prototype. The responses are held by the form owner and were not available to me at the time of submission, so no survey scores are reported here and none have been estimated. This report therefore rests on the qualitative evidence in Section 3. Survey averages can be added to this section once the data is shared.

## 3. Qualitative themes (n = 5 transcripts)

| Theme | Original website | Prototype | Mentioned by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual design | Over-minimal: tiny font, plain white, large empty areas | "Colourful, structured, attractive" | Original: 4/5 (Aanvi: "blunt"); Prototype: 5/5 |
| Language | English/Japanese mixed, no way to switch | One consistent language | Original: 5/5; Prototype: 4/5 |
| Product presentation | Flat product images only, can't see fit | Lookbook with models | 5/5 on both |
| Home page / navigation | Hard to find categories and products | Categories, collections and drops visible on home | 5/5 |
| Search | "Can't figure out where to search" (Nisarg) | Easy product search | 4/5 |
| Size selection and cart | Not discussed | Easy to select size and add to cart | 5/5 |
| Checkout | Not discussed | Simple, browser autofill helps, can keep shopping | 4/5 |
| Drop countdown | Not discussed | Valued for planning purchases of limited releases | 3/5 |
| Brand identity | Over-minimalism "ruins brand image" | Streetwear identity preserved | 3/5 |

**Link to earlier work.** Several findings match problems found earlier: no filters/search help (Critical Analysis: Search 4/10), no drop feedback (Feedback 5/10), no fit information (Persona pain point 2). The prototype's countdown, lookbook and search address these, and participants said so unprompted.

**Not mentioned by anyone** (so the transcripts give no evidence either way): sell-out and queue experience, size guide and fit notes, guest checkout, UPI/COD, order tracking, filters, text contrast, and form validation. Your Heuristic Evaluation scored contrast and validation as real problems, so these remain untested.

## 4. Honest conclusion
Based on qualitative evidence only (survey data unavailable).

Every participant preferred the prototype, and the reasons were consistent: models and lookbook, one language, easier discovery, simple checkout, and the drop countdown. On this evidence the redesign resolved the main frustrations people voiced about the original.

Limitations that weaken the claim:
- The recordings are retrospective reviews, not live Think Aloud sessions. No one narrated a task step by step, so there are no moments of hesitation or error to analyse.
- Three transcripts (Archita, Siddhi, Aditya) follow almost the same structure and phrasing, which suggests shared notes or a script. They may not count as independent observations.
- No participant raised a single problem with the prototype, which is unusual and suggests positivity bias (friends and batchmates).
- The prototype's own known weaknesses (see Heuristic Evaluation) were not probed.
- n = 5, no task-completion time or error data.
- No quantitative survey data is available, so the preference for the prototype is not confirmed by ratings.

Conclusion: based on the transcripts alone, the prototype likely improves first impressions, product visualisation and discoverability. Claims about usability of the full flow need a task-based retest with timing and errors.
